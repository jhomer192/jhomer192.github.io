Holdbreaker

waits on hold so you do not have to
Side project · 2026 · Private testing

A phone app that calls a company for you, works through the phone menu, waits on hold, and rings you the moment a person picks up. It is in private testing against a simulated phone line and has not called a real company yet.

Calling customer service means working through the phone menu, then forty minutes of hold music, then a person who picks up the moment you look away. Holdbreaker does the first two parts and hands you the third.

It is a connector, not a stand-in. It never argues your case or speaks for you; its only job is to get a person on the line and then get out of the way.

How it works

  • One box to start: type the company, the number and what you need, and Claude asks only for what is missing.
  • Menus without handing over your data: known menus are answered by rules and by playbooks shared between calls first. Claude chooses a menu option only through a narrow tool call, and account numbers are entered by code from an encrypted vault, so the model never sees them.
  • Hold detection runs on the server, combining voice-activity and hold-music signals, and needs several of them before it decides a person is on the line.
  • The handoff: it calls you from a saved contact and connects you when you press 1. Apart from a brief automated greeting that says it is an automated assistant, it does not talk to the representative.
  • Privacy: no audio is recorded, and transcription stops once a person may be on the line. A legal review comes before any public launch.

Built with

Next.js, installable as a web app, with a thin iPhone wrapper · a Python call worker · Postgres · Twilio Voice and Verify · Deepgram speech recognition · Claude Sonnet for intake and menu choices.

Not public yet Every call goes to a simulated line while it is tested. There is no public sign-up.
